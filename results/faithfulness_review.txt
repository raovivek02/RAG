- Hemorrhagic tendencies or blood dyscrasias [1]
- Recent or contemplated surgery of the central nervous system or eye, or traumatic surgery resulting in large open surfaces [1]
- Active ulceration or overt bleeding of the gastrointestinal, genitourinary, or respiratory tract [1]
- Central nervous system hemorrhage [1]
- Cerebral aneurysms, dissecting aorta [1]
- Pericarditis and pericardial effusions [1]
- Bacterial endocarditis [1]
- Threatened abortion, eclampsia, and preeclampsia [1]
- Unsupervised patients with conditions associated with potential high levels of non-compliance [1]
- Spinal puncture and other diagnostic or therapeutic procedures with potential for uncontrollable bleeding [1]
- Hypersensitivity to warfarin or any component of the product [3]
- Major regional or lumbar block anesthesia [3]
- Malignant hypertension [3]
- Pregnant women, except those with mechanical heart valves who are at high risk of thromboembolism [2,3]

Pregnant women with mechanical heart valves are an exception where the benefits may outweigh the risks, but warfarin sodium is generally contraindicated in pregnancy due to the risk of fetal harm [2].

##### Q: What is the boxed warning for tramadol about?
ANSWER: The boxed warning for tramadol hydrochloride extended-release tablets includes several key points:

1. **Serious and Life-Threatening Risks**: There is a risk of serious, life-threatening, or fatal respiratory depression, especially upon initiation or following a dosage increase [1, 2].

2. **Addiction, Abuse, and Misuse**: The use of tramadol exposes patients to risks of opioid addiction, abuse, and misuse, which can lead to overdose and death. Assessment of patient risk is necessary [1, 2].

3. **Management by Neonatology Experts**: Management by neonatology experts should be available at delivery for neonates exposed to tramadol [3].

4. **Opioid Analgesic Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS)**: Healthcare providers are encouraged to complete a REMS-compliant education program and to counsel patients and caregivers on serious risks, safe use, and the importance of reading the Medication Guide with each prescription [3].

5. **Ultra-Rapid Metabolism**: Life-threatening respiratory depression and death have occurred in children who received tramadol, particularly those with evidence of being ultra-rapid metabolizers of tramadol [3].

6. **Accidental Ingestion**: Accidental ingestion of tramadol hydrochloride extended-release tablets, especially by children, can result in a fatal overdose of tramadol [4, 5].

7. **Concomitant Use with CNS Depressants**: Concomitant use of opioids with benzodiazepines or other central nervous system depressants, including alcohol, may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death [4, 5].

##### Q: What is the recommended starting dose of levothyroxine?
